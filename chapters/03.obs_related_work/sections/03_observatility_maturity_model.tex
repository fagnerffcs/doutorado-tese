\section{The Observability Maturity Model (OMM)}
\label{sec:omm}

As a direct outcome of the Rapid Review conducted to address Specific Objective \textbf{SO4} and Secondary Research Question \textbf{SRQ4}, this research formalizes and proposes the \textit{Observability Maturity Model} (OMM). Maturity models are widely recognized in software engineering and information systems as structured instruments for assessing organizational capabilities, diagnosing architectural gaps, and guiding staged evolutionary improvements \cite{proenca_2016}. Within the Design Science Research (DSR) literature, maturity models must not be treated as informal checklists; rather, they constitute multi-dimensional \textbf{design artifacts} that demand rigorous conceptual grounding, mutually exclusive capability stages, and empirical validation \cite{mettler_2011, poppelbuss_2011}.

To establish a solid scientific foundation for the OMM, this research adopts a hybrid methodological framework combining the procedural design guidelines of \citeonline{becker_2009}, the design science perspective of \citeonline{mettler_2011}, and the six-stage developmental lifecycle model of \citeonline{debruin_2005}, building upon the observability capability foundations reviewed in Chapter~\ref{chap:observability} (Sections~\ref{sec:obs-time} and~\ref{sec:analytical-shift}) \cite{valbusa_2025, agarwal_2022, nethala_2025, patel_2025}.

Furthermore, leveraging the three purposes of use defined by \citeonline{debruin_2005} and \citeonline{mettler_2011}, the OMM is structured to provide three complementary utilities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Descriptive Utility:} Acts as a diagnostic instrument to assess the current ``as-is'' capability level of a Kubernetes cluster's telemetry pipeline, isolating unindexed log silos and missing distributed trace propagation.
    \item \textbf{Prescriptive Utility:} Provides an actionable architectural roadmap. The PANOPTES reference architecture serves as the prescriptive engineering vehicle designed to elevate a Kubernetes cluster from basic monitoring (Levels~1--2) to correlated multimodal observability (Level~4 -- Optimized).
    \item \textbf{Comparative Utility:} Enables quantitative benchmarking across deployment topologies by comparing diagnostic efficiency ($MTTDiag$ and $Acc$) against the operational resource footprint ($\Delta_{\text{CPU}}, \Delta_{\text{RAM}}$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Development Phases of OMM and Mitigating the Validation Gap}
To construct and validate the model, this work operationalizes the six-stage developmental lifecycle proposed by \citeonline{debruin_2005}, illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:model-dev-phases}:

    \caption{Maturity Model Development Lifecycle (De Bruin et al., 2005)}
    \label{fig:model-dev-phases}
    \par\medskip\ABNTEXfontereduzida\selectfont\textbf{Source:} \citeonline{debruin_2005} \par\medskip
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scope:} Defined as a domain-specific model focusing on cloud-native containerized applications orchestrated by Kubernetes, addressing the architectural gaps in correlating multi-signal telemetry data.
    
    \item \textbf{Design:} Employs a multi-dimensional, stage-based progression architecture. It defines the telemetry space formally as the tuple $O = \{M, L, T, E\}$---representing Metrics, Logs, Traces, and Events---and evaluates capability across three architectural dimensions: telemetry instrumentation scope, storage and cross-pillar correlation, and diagnostic capability.
    
    \item \textbf{Populate:} Achieved through a hybrid approach combining top-down theoretical stage definitions (Kalman's state reconstructibility) with bottom-up empirical capability extraction synthesized from the $N = 10$ primary studies (screened from $312$ initial records) in our Rapid Review.
    
    \item \textbf{Test (Mitigating the Validation Gap):} In a seminal systematic mapping study of $237$ maturity model publications, \citeonline{wendler_2012} identified a pervasive deficiency in the literature: \textbf{the Validation Gap}. The vast majority of academic maturity models are published with speculative or purely qualitative self-assessments, lacking repeatable, quantitative empirical validation of their prescribed target states. Our research directly addresses this Validation Gap by subjecting the model's target state (\textbf{Level~4 -- Optimized}) to a controlled, automated $2\times2$ factorial experiment under Chaos Engineering (Chapters~\ref{chap:experiment-implementation} and~\ref{chap:results-and-discussion}). Across $N=30$ paired fault-injection trials per scenario, we provide statistically rigorous evidence (via Mann-Whitney U, Wilcoxon Signed-Rank, Fisher's Exact, and McNemar's tests) that transitioning from Level~2 (Vanilla CLI baseline) and Level~3 (monomodal tracing) to Level~4 (PANOPTES correlated multimodal observability) drastically reduces $MTTDiag$ and elevates diagnostic accuracy ($Acc$).
    
    \item \textbf{Deploy:} Instantiated and shared as reproducible open-source Infrastructure as Code artifacts (K3d/Multipass provisioning scripts, Ansible playbooks, Helm charts, and Python RCA harnesses), enabling deterministic replication.
    
    \item \textbf{Maintain:} Engineered with an extensible capability schema where Level~4 provides the clean, semantically correlated data substrate required to train and operate Level~5 (Predictive/Autonomic AIOps) models.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{OMM Evolutionary Levels and Architecture Matrix}
As a result of the \textit{Scope}, \textit{Design}, and \textit{Populate} phases, Table~\ref{tab:omm_architecture} formalizes the Observability Maturity Model (OMM) across five cumulative capability levels, mapping the operational progression of the telemetry tuple $O = \{M, L, T, E\}$ against concrete Kubernetes instrumentation patterns and supporting evidence from the Rapid Review.

\input{chapters/03.obs_related_work/tables/tbl-omm-arch}

The five evolutionary stages are characterized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Level 1 -- Non-Existent:} The system operates as an opaque black box ($O = \emptyset$). Containers emit only ephemeral \texttt{stdout} streams lost upon pod eviction or crash. Troubleshooting is purely reactive, relying on user outage reports and manual container restarts \cite{kosinska_2023, usman_2022}.
    
    \item \textbf{Level 2 -- Initial / Ad-Hoc:} Characterized by siloed, localized telemetry ($O = \{M_{\text{local}}, L_{\text{local}}\}$) and the absence of distributed traces ($T = \emptyset$). Basic node/pod metrics (e.g., Kubernetes \texttt{metrics-server} / \texttt{cAdvisor}) and unindexed container logs are inspected on demand via CLI commands (\texttt{kubectl top/logs}). Isolating cascading microservice failures requires sequential, unindexed log scraping, resulting in high cognitive load and prolonged diagnostic latency \cite{zanatta_23, faseeha_2025}.
    
    \item \textbf{Level 3 -- Managed (Centralized Silos):} Multi-pillar collection is centralized into dedicated storage backends ($O = \{M_{\text{central}}, L_{\text{central}}, T_{\text{siloed}}\}$), such as Prometheus, Loki, and Jaeger/Tempo. However, telemetry modalities remain semantically unlinked or are inspected monomodally in isolation. Operators must manually pivot across disparate query interfaces or rely on naive total-duration trace sorting, which suffers from upstream cascading span bias \cite{araujo_2024, hartikainen_2024, wei_2025}.
    
    \item \textbf{Level 4 -- Optimized (Correlated --- The PANOPTES Target):} Full multimodal semantic correlation is operationalized ($O = \{M \bowtie L \bowtie T \bowtie E\}$). The pipeline enforces unified OpenTelemetry resource attributes, W3C Trace Context propagation, exemplar bindings from metrics to traces, contextual trace-to-log mappings, and exclusive span self-time decomposition ($T_{\text{self}} = T_{\text{span}} - \sum T_{\text{children}}$) \cite{faseeha_2025, gomes_2025, hartikainen_2024, kosinska_2023}. Empirical validation of this level's diagnostic superiority and bounded overhead is the central experimental contribution of this thesis.
    
    \item \textbf{Level 5 -- Predictive / Autonomic (AIOps):} The correlated telemetry stream feeds autonomous closed-loop control agents ($O = f(M, L, T, E) \rightarrow \text{Control Action}$). Graph Attention Networks (GATs) \cite{han_2024}, causal inference models, and zero-code kernel probes (eBPF) process the high-fidelity Level~4 substrate to anticipate SLO violations, trigger proactive autoscaling, and execute automated self-healing and GreenOps/SecOps policies \cite{deng_2024, gomes_2025}.
\end{enumerate}
