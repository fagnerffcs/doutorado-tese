\section{The Observability Maturity Model (OMM)}
\label{sec:omm}

As a direct outcome of the Rapid Review conducted to address SO4, this research formalizes Observability ($O$) as a multidimensional set of telemetry data defined as $O = \{M, L, T, E\}$, where M, L, T, and E represent Metrics, Logs, Traces, and Events, respectively.

Maturity models are widely recognized in software engineering and IT service management as effective instruments for assessing organizational capabilities and guiding staged improvements. It is a technique that has been proved to be valuable in measuring different aspects of a
process or an organization \cite{proenca_2016}.

To ensure the methodological rigor in proposing a novel model for this domain, it is necessary to understand the phases related to the creation of the Observability Maturity Model (OMM). According to \cite{debruin_2005}  the development framework outlines six generic phases as seen in figure \ref{fig:model-dev-phases}, which we characterize within the context of this study as follows:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scope:} The model is domain-specific, focusing exclusively on cloud-native environments orchestrated by Kubernetes to address the lack of unified telemetry standards.
    \item \textbf{Design:} The OMM is designed to represent maturity as a series of cumulative, one-dimensional linear stages. Its primary audience comprises Site Reliability Engineers (SREs), DevOps practitioners, and researchers seeking to evaluate and improve system controllability.
    \item \textbf{Populate:} The identification of the evolutionary stages and domain components was achieved through the synthesis of findings from the Rapid Review and existing distributed systems literature.
    \item \textbf{Test:} The construct validity of the model's target state is tested empirically through the controlled experiment (Chaos Engineering), evaluating the operational effectiveness of the PANOPTES architecture instantiation.
    \item \textbf{Deploy:} The practical instantiation of the model is deployed and shared with the community as a reproducible open-source artifact (Infrastructure as Code), enabling its application by external entities.
    \item \textbf{Maintain:} The OMM establishes a foundational roadmap that anticipates future evolution, particularly the continuous integration of predictive modeling and AIOps as the domain matures.
\end{itemize}

As a result of the \textit{Scope}, \textit{Design}, and \textit{Populate} phases, we propose the OMM organized into five evolutionary levels:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Level 1 - Non-Existent:} Observability is not implemented; no logs, traces, or metrics are systematically collected.
    \item \textbf{Level 2 - Initial/Ad-Hoc:} Basic logs and metrics are collected but remain siloed in local storage. Traces are generally absent, making correlation a manual and inefficient process.
    \item \textbf{Level 3 - Managed:} Characterized by the automated, centralized collection and aggregation of logs, traces, and metrics. Basic dashboards are available for global system analysis.
    \item \textbf{Level 4 - Optimized:} Advanced visual analytics and semantically integrated dashboards provide actionable insights. The architecture allows for proactive issue detection and rapid resolution (the target state of the PANOPTES architecture).
    \item \textbf{Level 5 - Predictive:} The highest stage, where AI-driven agents, predictive modeling, and machine learning algorithms analyze the telemetry set $O$ to anticipate and prevent failures before they impact the environment.
\end{enumerate}

By defining this model early in the research, the PANOPTES architecture is conceptually positioned as the engineering vehicle designed to elevate a Kubernetes cluster from Level 2 (Initial/Ad-Hoc) to Level 4 (Optimized), setting the foundational data structures required for future Level 5 (Predictive) AIOps implementations.
