\section{Rapid Review Methodology}
\label{sec:rapid-review}

To map how industry and academia are addressing the analytical shift toward observability in Kubernetes-based environments, we conducted a Rapid Review (RR). Rapid Reviews are secondary studies designed to provide timely, rigorous research evidence to support architectural decision-making in software engineering environments without the multi-year latency of a traditional Systematic Literature Review \cite{cartaxo_2018}.

\subsection{Rapid Review Protocol and Thesis Alignment}

Rapid Reviews are conducted considering operational constraints while preserving protocol transparency, systematic search strings, and explicit inclusion/exclusion filtering \cite{cartaxo_2018}. This approach is particularly suitable for cloud-native observability, a rapidly evolving domain where toolchains and CNCF standards advance continuously. Similar to traditional Systematic Reviews (SRs), this Rapid Review was structured into three sequential phases: planning, performing, and reporting.

The execution of this Rapid Review, initially published as an independent study \cite{silva_2026}, served as the foundational investigative step (Relevance and Rigor Cycles) for this thesis. There is a direct structural alignment between the research questions of this review (\textbf{RQ01}--\textbf{RQ04}) and the Secondary Research Questions (\textbf{SRQ1}--\textbf{SRQ4}) of this doctoral research:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Theoretical and Technological Foundation (RQ01 and RQ03 $\rightarrow$ SRQ1):} The investigation into essential architectural components (\textbf{RQ01}) and open-source toolchains (\textbf{RQ03}) provided the empirical literature backing to answer \textbf{SRQ1}, establishing the required technological stack (Metrics, Logs, and Traces via OpenTelemetry, Prometheus, Loki, and Tempo).
    \item \textbf{Gap Identification and Maturity Modeling (RQ02 and RQ04 $\rightarrow$ SRQ4):} The systematic search for existing reference architectures (\textbf{RQ02}) and the synthesis of architectural fragmentation and overhead gaps (\textbf{RQ04}) directly addressed \textbf{SRQ4}, culminating in the formalization of the Observability Maturity Model (OMM) in Section~\ref{sec:omm}.
\end{itemize}

Ultimately, while this Rapid Review establishes the theoretical boundaries of the domain (\textbf{SRQ1} and \textbf{SRQ4}), it acts as the springboard for the constructive and empirical contributions of this thesis (\textbf{SRQ2} and \textbf{SRQ3})---specifically, the instantiation of the PANOPTES reference architecture and its quantitative validation under Chaos Engineering.

\subsection{Planning Phase and Research Questions}
In the planning phase, we followed the protocol guidelines established by Cartaxo \textit{et al.} \cite{cartaxo_2018}, encompassing problem formulation, review research questions, search strategy, selection criteria, quality assessment, and data extraction procedures. The online platform Parsifal was utilized to manage all stages of the protocol, ensuring auditability and methodological rigor.

Cloud-native environments, characterized by microservices, ephemeral containers, and dynamic orchestration (Kubernetes), generate an exponentially growing volume, variety, and velocity of telemetry data (metrics, logs, traces, and events). As highlighted by \cite{sridharan_2018}, managing and correlating this raw telemetry without a cohesive architecture becomes technically unfeasible and economically prohibitive, constraining a system's capacity to diagnose ``unknown unknowns.''

To guide this Rapid Review, four review research questions were formulated:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ01:} What are the essential components of an observability architecture for Kubernetes-based cloud-native applications?
    \item \textbf{RQ02:} What reference architectures or observability frameworks for Kubernetes-based cloud-native environments have been proposed in the literature?
    \item \textbf{RQ03:} What open-source tools are used or recommended to implement the different components of a Kubernetes-based observability architecture for cloud-native applications?
    \item \textbf{RQ04:} What research gaps and future directions have been identified in the literature on open-source observability architectures for Kubernetes-based cloud-native applications?
\end{itemize}

The literature search was executed across four leading scientific databases in Computer Science and Software Engineering: IEEE Xplore ($114$ records), ACM Digital Library ($88$ records), SpringerLink ($68$ records), and ScienceDirect ($42$ records), yielding $N = 312$ initial records published between January 2018 and October 2025.

The search string, adapted to each database's full-text syntax, was defined as:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Search String:} (``cloud native'' OR ``cloud-native'' OR ``microservices'') AND (``observability'' OR ``monitoring'' OR ``tracing'' OR ``logging'' OR ``metrics'' OR ``events'') AND (``kubernetes'' OR ``k8s'') AND (``open source'' OR ``open-source'')
\end{quote}

Following deduplication ($58$ duplicates removed, leaving $254$ unique records) and title/abstract screening ($46$ candidate records retained for full-text evaluation), $8$ primary studies were selected directly from the indexed databases. Using seminal studies such as \cite{kosinska_2023} and \cite{usman_2022} as seeds, a complementary forward and backward citation network expansion (snowballing assisted by Research Rabbit and Connected Papers) identified $2$ additional high-relevance technical studies \cite{araujo_2024, hartikainen_2024} that satisfied quality assessment thresholds ($\text{QA} \ge 3.5$) by providing direct empirical benchmarks of OpenTelemetry and LGTM stacks in Kubernetes, resulting in a final synthesis corpus of $N = 10$ primary studies.

Six Inclusion Criteria (IC) governed selection: \textbf{IC01} -- Primary focus on Observability for software systems; \textbf{IC02} -- Context related to cloud-native applications or microservices; \textbf{IC03} -- Discussion of Kubernetes as the orchestration platform; \textbf{IC04} -- Focus on open-source observability tools or architectures; \textbf{IC05} -- Studies published from January 2018 to October 2025; and \textbf{IC06} -- Studies written in English.

Conversely, six Exclusion Criteria (EC) were enforced during database screening: \textbf{EC01} -- Duplicated records; \textbf{EC02} -- Non-archival gray literature (e.g., commercial blog posts or vendor marketing whitepapers); \textbf{EC03} -- Focus exclusively on traditional infrastructure monitoring without addressing observability concepts; \textbf{EC04} -- Studies where observability or Kubernetes is mentioned only tangentially; \textbf{EC05} -- Studies exclusively evaluating proprietary/closed-source SaaS tools without open-source baselines; and \textbf{EC06} -- Extended abstracts, posters, or non-empirical summaries.

For each of the $N = 10$ included primary studies, data extraction was performed in Parsifal across the four review dimensions: essential architectural layers and telemetry types (\textbf{RQ01}); reference frameworks and deployment patterns (\textbf{RQ02}); open-source toolchains and interoperability constraints (\textbf{RQ03}); and open research gaps and future directions (\textbf{RQ04}).
