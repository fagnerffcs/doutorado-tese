\section{Validation of Design Science Control Principles and Heuristics}
\label{sec:dsr-validation}

A fundamental requirement of the Design Science Research (DSR) methodology is demonstrating that the theoretical constructs formulated in the Rigor and Design Cycles are tangibly materialized in the instantiated artifact and validated by empirical evidence \cite{hevner_2004, gregor_2013}. The experimental results across Scenarios~1 through~4b directly validate the Control Principles (\texttt{CP1}--\texttt{CP2}) and Contingency Heuristics (\texttt{ConH1}--\texttt{ConH3}) proposed in Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology}:

\textbf{Materialization of Control Principles}

The Control Principles define the structural axioms required for the telemetry pipeline to operate as a deterministic state-reconstruction feedback loop:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CP1 (Principle of Causal Linkage):} Postulates that state attribution in distributed microservices is impossible without explicit temporal and topological bindings across telemetry modalities. This principle was empirically validated by the stark contrast between Scenario~4a ($0.00\%$ accuracy under isolated monomodal trace inspection) and Scenario~4b ($83.33\%$ accuracy under multimodal correlation). By linking Prometheus container saturation and pod-reset metrics, Loki structured exception logs (with regex-extracted \texttt{traceID}s), and Tempo distributed traces decomposed via exclusive span self-time ($T_{\text{self}} = T_{\text{span}} - \sum T_{\text{children}}$) and L4/L7 peer attributes (\textbf{ADR-0002}), PANOPTES eliminated the upstream cascading span bias that crippled monomodal tracing.
    
    \item \textbf{CP2 (Principle of Unified State Representation):} Postulates that heterogeneous signals ($O = \{M, L, T, E\}$) must share a normalized semantic schema to enable programmatic graph traversal without vendor lock-in. This was validated by the OpenTelemetry Collector DaemonSet pipeline and unified Kubernetes label taxonomy (\texttt{namespace}, \texttt{pod}, \texttt{service.name}), which enabled our deterministic RCA engine in Scenario~4b to query Prometheus, Loki, and Tempo over standard HTTP APIs and achieve $100\%$ accuracy on both \texttt{cpu-stress} ($11/11$) and \texttt{pod-kill} ($9/9$) perturbations without invoking a single \texttt{kubectl} CLI command.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Materialization of Contingency Heuristics}

The Contingency Heuristics prescribe context-aware operational rules that preserve internal validity and cluster stability:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ConH1 (Heuristic of Declarative Control):} Mandates automated reconciliation and dynamic service discovery over manual configuration to minimize control delay. This was validated through declarative Helm charts, Prometheus \texttt{ServiceMonitor} CRDs, and the automated pre-flight health checks in Scenario~4b, which immediately tracked newly rescheduled pods following \texttt{pod-kill} events (converging in a median of $3.16\,\text{s}$).
    
    \item \textbf{ConH2 (Heuristic of Configuration Drift):} Treats infrastructure and state drift as external disturbances that must be rejected. Codifying the 3-node K3d cluster with hard Linux \texttt{cgroup} quotas (\textbf{ADR-0006}), deterministic Chaos Mesh finalizer cleanup (\textbf{ADR-0004}), and seeded paired trial schedules (\texttt{RANDOM\_SEED = 42}, \textbf{ADR-0001}) yielded virtually identical statistical distributions between Scenario~3 ($Acc = 20.00\%$) and its independent replication in Scenario~3-Stratified ($Acc = 23.33\%, \; p = 1.000$), proving zero configuration drift across campaigns.
    
    \item \textbf{ConH3 (Heuristic of Adaptive Feedback and Bounded Overhead):} Restricts the operational footprint of the observability stack to prevent observer-induced destabilization. Combining workload pruning of non-transactional bloat pods (\textbf{ADR-0005}) with OTel batch processing, Loki label-only indexing, and Tempo index-free object blocks kept the dynamic CPU footprint of PANOPTES at just $\mathbf{9.51\% \pm 2.14\%}$ of the monitored workload ($189.47\,\text{mCPU}$ vs.\ $1{,}994.43\,\text{mCPU}$, $p = 6.52 \times 10^{-9}$) and its resident memory within $\mathbf{26.33\%}$ of the $12\,\text{GiB}$ cluster capacity ($p = 9.31 \times 10^{-10}$), validating Hypothesis $H_{1,3}$.
\end{itemize}
