\section{Vanilla Diagnostic Performance and Censorship Analysis (Scenario 3 and Scenario 3-Stratified)}
\label{sec:scenario3-results}

Scenario~3 established the diagnostic control baseline for the experiment, evaluating the efficacy of an automated Root Cause Analysis (RCA) heuristic restricted exclusively to native Kubernetes CLI inspection (\texttt{kubectl get pods}, \texttt{kubectl logs --since-time=}$T_0$, and \texttt{kubectl top pods}). To ensure high internal validity and verify test-retest reproducibility on the Vanilla cluster, two full 30-trial campaigns were executed under the identical seeded fault schedule (\texttt{RANDOM\_SEED = 42}; $11$ \texttt{cpu-stress}, $10$ \texttt{network-delay}, and $9$ \texttt{pod-kill} trials across $\mathcal{P}_{\text{target}} = \{\texttt{cart}, \texttt{currency}, \texttt{payment}, \texttt{shipping}\}$): the primary baseline (\textbf{Scenario~3}, persisted in \texttt{scenario3\_diagnostic\_trials.csv}) and an independent stratified replication (\textbf{Scenario~3-Stratified}, persisted in \texttt{scenario3\_diagnostic\_trials\_stratified.csv}).

\subsection{Censorship Rate, Silent Degradation, and Test-Retest Consistency}

The empirical results across both 30-trial campaigns revealed a severe, structural diagnostic limitation inherent to native CLI and log-scraping workflows in Kubernetes microservices. As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:scenario3_outcomes}, the Vanilla agent in Scenario~3 suffered an overwhelming right-censorship rate of $\mathbf{80.00\%}$ ($24$ out of $30$ trials timing out at $300.0\,\text{s}$), achieving a diagnostic accuracy of only $\mathbf{20.00\%}$ ($6/30$ successes; median $MTTDiag = 300.00\,\text{s}$, mean $= 242.42 \pm 117.18\,\text{s}$).

The independent replication in Scenario~3-Stratified yielded virtually identical performance: a right-censorship rate of $\mathbf{73.33\%}$ ($22/30$ timeouts), $1$ misattribution ($3.33\%$, Trial~2 where a \texttt{pod-kill} on \texttt{currency} was misattributed to \texttt{shipping} at $27.72\,\text{s}$), and a diagnostic accuracy of $\mathbf{23.33\%}$ ($7/30$ successes; median $MTTDiag = 300.00\,\text{s}$, mean $= 224.12 \pm 128.16\,\text{s}$). A two-sided Fisher's Exact Test ($p = 1.000$) and Mann-Whitney $U$ test ($U = 474.5, \; p = 0.655$) between Scenario~3 and Scenario~3-Stratified confirmed zero statistically significant divergence between the two runs, proving the determinism of the Vanilla testbed.

The $73.3\%\text{--}80.0\%$ timeout rate is a direct manifestation of \textit{silent performance degradation} and \textit{ephemeral log loss} across the three fault classes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Total Blindness to Resource Saturation (\texttt{cpu-stress} --- $0/11$ in both runs):} When $100\%$ cgroup CPU throttling was injected into any of the four target microservices ($n=11$ trials), the containers did not crash or emit exception stack traces to \texttt{stdout/stderr}; they simply processed requests more slowly. Consequently, log-based regex polling failed in $100\%$ of \texttt{cpu-stress} trials ($MTTDiag = 300.0\,\text{s}$ across all $11$ trials in both S3 and S3-Stratified).
    \item \textbf{Ephemeral Evidence Loss on Container Termination (\texttt{pod-kill} --- $3/9$ in S3, $4/9$ in S3-Stratified):} When Chaos Mesh abruptly killed a pod (e.g., \texttt{currency} in Trials~2 and~7, or \texttt{payment} in Trials~15 and~20), the dying container's local stdout buffer was destroyed or rotated by the kubelet before sequential CLI polling reached it, unless an upstream caller (\texttt{frontend} or \texttt{checkout}) happened to log an explicit connection-refused string naming the peer (which occurred consistently only when \texttt{cart} was killed in Trials~19, 24, and~25, converging in $4.95\text{--}5.96\,\text{s}$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Asymmetric Success and Protocol Boundaries in Network Degradations}

Across the $n=10$ \texttt{network-delay} trials ($2000\,\text{ms}$ latency), both Scenario~3 and Scenario~3-Stratified achieved an identical $30.00\%$ success rate ($3/10$), succeeding exclusively when the fault targeted the \texttt{shipping} service (Trials~12, 18, and~26, converging in $11.92\text{--}22.96\,\text{s}$ in S3 and $12.32\text{--}42.54\,\text{s}$ in S3-Stratified) while timing out at $300.0\,\text{s}$ on every \texttt{cart}, \texttt{currency}, and \texttt{payment} network delay.

This asymmetric behavior stems from protocol-specific timeout thresholds in the Astronomy Shop call graph. When a $2000\,\text{ms}$ delay is injected into \texttt{shipping}, the upstream HTTP client inside \texttt{frontend}/\texttt{checkout} breaches its hardcoded request deadline and emits an explicit HTTP error log (\texttt{"failed POST to shipping"}). Conversely, gRPC calls to \texttt{cart}, \texttt{currency}, and \texttt{payment} tolerate the $2000\,\text{ms}$ delay within their default gRPC context deadlines, absorbing the latency silently without writing error strings to \texttt{stdout}. This proves that native Kubernetes CLI and log inspection (\textbf{OMM Level~2}) is structurally incapable of diagnosing non-crashing latency and resource saturation faults.
